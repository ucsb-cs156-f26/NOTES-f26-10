# Section 4.1: Differences in Scope

Summary by Nathan

# Question 1
Most programming assignments in my CS courses have clearly defined requirements, which allows me to focus primarily on implementation rather than determining the scope of the problem. During my internship, however, I worked on a transcription project where many aspects of the system were still being overhauled. This required independent research, evaluating different approaches, and making technical decisions without a predefined solution. Although some of my project-based courses have provided similar experiences, my internship demonstrated how much more uncertainty can exist in real world software development.
# Question 2
One major difference developers encountered was the lack of clearly defined requirements in industry. Unlike school assignments, where the expected behavior and deliverables are usually specified in advance, industry projects may begin before the problem is fully understood. Developers also found that requirements often involve discussions between engineers and product managers to balance technical limitations with product goals. Additionally, industry projects require consideration of factors such as scalability, fault tolerance, and deployment, which receive less attention in typical coursework.
# Question 3
The paper suggests that instructors could change project requirements after students have already begun implementation, allowing them to practice adapting to unexpected changes. It also emphasizes the value of project-based courses, where students must manage deadlines and determine realistic project scopes. My own suggestion would be to incorporate more open-ended assignments that require students to research potential solutions, evaluate tradeoffs, and establish their own implementation plans. This would provide more experience with the uncertainty and decision-making involved in industry development.
